01 · A legal share

Tokens in the company that owns the building.

Each property is held by its own LLC. Your tokens are shares in that entity — a real, recorded ownership stake, not an IOU or a pooled fund.

02 · Monthly rent

Your share of the net rent, every month.

After expenses and reserves, the building's rent is distributed to owners in USDC — proportional to what you hold, paid on a predictable monthly schedule.

03 · A share of the upside

And your share when the property sells.

If the building is sold, you receive your share of the proceeds after debts and fees. Until then, you can list your tokens on the secondary market — though liquidity is never guaranteed.

Straight answers

Questions worth asking.

Tokens representing a share in the LLC that owns a specific building. That share entitles you to a proportional cut of the property's net rental income and, if it's ever sold, the sale proceeds after debts and fees. It is a real ownership stake — not a loan, a fund, or a promise.

Rent is collected, expenses and reserves are paid, and the remainder is distributed to owners in USDC on a monthly schedule — proportional to what you hold. You can reinvest distributions or withdraw them at any time.

Yes — you can list your tokens on Vesper's secondary market, with no lockup on most properties. That said, a buyer has to be willing to purchase at your price, so liquidity is never guaranteed and your order may not fill immediately.
